About

I am an economist working on environmental and resource economics, energy economics, and experimental and behavioral economics.

My research uses induced-value auctions, discrete choice experiments, field experiments, and large-scale online experiments to study how people respond to environmental policies — especially the roles of social norms, goal setting, reference dependence, and social recognition. I received my PhD in Economics from the University of Wyoming in 2014, advised by Jason Shogren. Before joining Xi'an Eurasia University, I was Associate Professor and PhD supervisor at the Jinhe Center for Economic Research, Xi'an Jiaotong University.

I have 12 peer-reviewed articles and chapters, including in Energy Policy, Ecological Economics, Journal of Economic Psychology, and China & World Economy. Several of my experimental papers have been included in the University of Cambridge YNOT Behavioural Policy Programme and the University of Pennsylvania's Choice Construction Lab.

Research

My work asks how non-price interventions change environmental behavior, and why their effects often fade. I combine controlled experiments with field data to separate the mechanisms behind persistence, motivation crowding, and policy response.

Behavioral environmental economics

Social norms, goal setting, social recognition, and reference dependence: how these mechanisms shape energy and resource use, and when their effects survive after an intervention stops.

Energy and climate policy

Field and online experiments on household energy conservation, climate change contribution, time preference, and intergenerational responsibility. Recent work examines private versus public information about goals in student dormitories.

Payments for ecosystem services and mechanism design

Willingness-to-accept versus willingness-to-pay disparities, promise scripts, motivational crowding, and the design of incentive-compatible contracts for environmental protection.
